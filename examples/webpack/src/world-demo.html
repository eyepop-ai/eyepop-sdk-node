▸ Feature

Point cloud is a Pop with nothing in it but a depth map asking for toWorld, on the large landscape ability: no detector runs, the map itself is back-projected, and the whole scene comes back as one cloud. That is also what reveals the map, so the video tab draws it as a heatmap. It is the heaviest of the three - a 924 x 518 grid is roughly half a million points per frame, which a still carries comfortably and a live webcam does not.

Person point cloud keeps the small landscape map hidden and puts a segmenter behind a person detector instead, so only the people come back as clouds. A component finder splits each mask into pieces; erode shrinks a mask before it is split, which is what stops two objects that touch by a few pixels from being read as one. At 0 the finder is left out altogether and the mask stays whole. Erode applies to this feature alone - the other two have no mask to shrink.

Hand distance runs 2d body points behind the same detector and measures between the two wrists. A mask is not drawn over the video in any mode - the 3D tab is where it becomes a cloud.

degrees

Normalised to the frame rather than given in pixels: fx and fy are the focal length as a fraction of the frame's width and height, cx and cy are the principal point in the same units. One calibration then survives a resolution change.

These are computed, not measured. A browser reports a camera's resolution but never its focal length, so the values above come from the field of view assumed at the top - the frame size only fixes the ratio between fx and fy. For a real measurement, calibrate the lens (OpenCV's checkerboard) and type the four numbers in. Applied when a webcam connects or a file is processed: change them and the loaded file is run again, while a live webcam has to be disconnected and connected once more. Values typed by hand are left alone when a new source reports its own resolution - only derived ones are recomputed.

A wrong field of view mostly cancels out of a hand to hand distance. Canonical metric depth scales with the focal length, so it cancels from the lateral coordinates and survives only along the optical axis - two hands held up side by side measure right even with the guess above, while one hand reached toward the camera does not.

A pose is entered as yaw, tilt and height because a unit quaternion is not something to type by hand; the one actually sent is shown beside them. Yaw turns about the world's up axis, counter-clockwise seen from above, so +90 degrees points the camera along world -X. Left off, the worker returns coordinates in the camera's own frame - OpenCV convention, X right, Y down, Z forward - rather than the world frame with Z up and the ground at Z = 0.

A pose moves and turns the whole scene, so it does not change the hand to hand distance: a rigid transform preserves distances. It changes where the points sit in the 3D tab, and nothing else. Applied like the intrinsics.
