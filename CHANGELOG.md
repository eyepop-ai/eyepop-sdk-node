# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- World coordinates on predictions. `worldX`, `worldY` and `worldZ` (metres) now deserialise on key points, outline points and contour points, including cutouts - they are members of `Point2d`, so every carrier the worker enriches inherits them. Prediction v2 only, and a point the worker could not place carries none of the three rather than a zero or a NaN, so test for `undefined`. `Point3d.z` is untouched and independent: `z` is model-relative depth in whatever convention the model uses, `worldZ` is metres.
- `examples/node/pop_demo.ts --toWorld` adds world coordinates to any of its example pops, composed or flat: it names a depth ability and walks the component tree - nested `forward` targets included - opting in every component that can carry them. Hidden components are skipped, since their predictions never reach the response, while their targets are still walked. `--depthMapToWorld` asks for the scene cloud instead, or as well, and stands on its own: given neither a pop nor a model it builds the scene-only Pop rather than refusing for want of something to run. `--cameraHfovDegrees` or `--cameraIntrinsics`, with optional `--cameraRotation` and `--cameraTranslation`, supply the source calibration, and without one the demo says which guess the worker is falling back to. `--output` summarizes `world` payloads instead of dumping megabytes of base64, and reports how many points came back placed - which is what separates a calibration that worked from one that quietly did not. `--worldOut scene.ply` writes everything in the results that carries world coordinates to an ASCII PLY in metres - key points, outlines, contours with their cutouts, mask point clouds and the scene cloud alike, so a pop that produces no masks still has something to write. `cloudsOfPrediction()` finds the dense clouds only; the sparse carriers hold `worldX`/`worldY`/`worldZ` on the points themselves, and the demo walks those too, labelling each series by class and carrier and numbering a class that appears more than once. One colour per series, with the labels as header comments since a PLY has no notion of a series, and the points are joined by `element edge`: outlines and contours as closed rings in the order the worker emitted them, key points as skeletons through the renderer's own `POSE_CONNECTIONS`. A pair with an unplaced point in it is skipped rather than bridged, so a partially placed contour breaks where the hole is instead of drawing a line the geometry does not have. Viewers that ignore PLY edges show the points exactly as before. Points the worker could not place are dropped rather than written as the NaN a viewer reads as a corrupt vertex, and coordinates are rounded to a tenth of a millimetre, which halves the file and costs nothing a depth model could resolve. Open it in MeshLab, CloudCompare or Blender. There is still no node counterpart to the python `--visualize-world` plot: that one draws through `EyePopWorldPlot` in the SDK, and the node SDK has no world plotting API to draw through.
- `Mask.world`, the dense per-object point cloud, with `decodePointCloud()` and `cloudOfObject()` to read it: one xyz triple per mask pixel, indexed exactly like the bitmap. `PointCloud.at(i, j)` samples by mask pixel and `atSource(x, y)` by source coordinate, since the mask spans the object's bounding box. NaN is the wire's omission sentinel here and is preserved, unlike `decodeDepthMap()`, which rejects a malformed payload but has no NaN to keep.
- `Pop.depthMap` (`PopDepthMap`, with `ability` or `abilityUuid`) names the depth ability whose map the worker back-projects through, and `toWorld` on a component selects whose predictions get translated. `validatePop()` rejects a depth map naming neither or both, locally, rather than leaving it to come back as a 400.
- `PopDepthMap.toWorld` back-projects the map itself, so the results carry a point cloud of the whole scene rather than one per segmented object. It is also what reveals the map: without it the depth branch the Pop builds stays out of the response. It stands on its own - a Pop with nothing but a `depthMap` asking for `toWorld` is complete, no component has to opt in.
- `Depth.world`, the scene point cloud, read by `cloudOfDepth(depth, sourceWidth, sourceHeight)`. Same encoding as `Mask.world` but on the depth map's own grid, so `at(i, j)` lands on the index that pixel's value sits at in `Depth.values`. Both members ship: a NaN point says only that a pixel could not be placed, while the value at that index says why - `+Infinity` for sky, or a reading that is not a distance at all. `cloudsOfPrediction()` returns every cloud in one prediction, scene last.
- `Depth.semantic` says what a depth map's values mean: `canonical_metric`, `metric`, `relative` or `unknown`. Always present in prediction v2, so an absent member means a worker that predates the field rather than a map that declined to say. Only the two metric flavours can be back-projected.
- Camera calibration per source: a `camera` on `ProcessParams`, and in the Pop's `defaults`. `Camera` carries normalised intrinsics (`fx`, `fy`, `cx`, `cy` as fractions of the frame rather than pixels, so one calibration survives a resolution change) or an `hfovDegrees` shorthand, the OpenCV distortion coefficients, and a camera-to-world pose as a unit quaternion plus a translation in metres. Exactly one of `intrinsics` and `hfovDegrees` is required; both is rejected rather than resolved by precedence. `validateCamera()` runs before a source is submitted, so a bad calibration is an error locally rather than a 400 halfway through an upload. Without one the worker assumes a 60 degree field of view, which leaves lateral measurements exact and every distance along the optical axis wrong by however wrong that guess is.
- Frame-level `depth` prediction member (`Depth` type) as produced by depth estimation abilities (e.g. `eyepop.depth.*`): base64 little-endian float32 map with the source frame's aspect ratio, sky pixels as `+Infinity`. `decodeDepthMap()` returns a `DepthMap` with typed values, finite min/max, and proportional source-coordinate sampling (`at()`, `isSky()`).
- `examples/web/static/hand-distance.html`, a browser demo of world coordinates end to end: webcam preview, camera selector, and a calibration panel, running 2d body points back-projected through `eyepop.depth.large` and overlaying the distance in metres between each person's wrists, live, with a line joining them. The wrists stand in for the hands - the 2D body model has no hand point of its own. The Pop names a `depthMap` without `toWorld`, so the worker builds the depth branch and keeps it out of the response: on a live stream that is the difference between a few key points per frame and a megabyte of base64. Intrinsics are prefilled from the resolution the camera reports and an assumed field of view, using the same arithmetic the worker applies to the `hfovDegrees` shorthand, and the page says outright that they are computed rather than measured, since a browser never reports a focal length. `validateCamera()` runs before the stream starts, and a pose whose wrists were found but could not be placed is drawn dashed rather than dropped, so missing depth reads differently from a missing person.
- `POSE_2D_CONNECTIONS`, `POSE_3D_CONNECTIONS`, `POSE_CONNECTIONS` and the `POSE_2D_CATEGORY` / `POSE_3D_CATEGORY` labels are exported from `@eyepop.ai/eyepop-render-2d`. They were module-private, so anyone drawing key points where the 2D renderer cannot reach - a 3D scene, a point cloud export, another canvas - had to copy the joint table, which is how two drawings of one skeleton come to disagree about where a wrist is. Joints are pairs of class labels rather than indices, so a model that emits its points in another order still draws correctly.
- `Render2d.renderDepth()`: turbo-colormap depth heatmap overlay (near = warm, far = cool, sky untouched by default), portable across node-canvas, browser, and react-native canvases.
- The `Mask` type is now exported from `@eyepop.ai/eyepop`.

### Fixed

- Compute session connection errors now preserve structured pipeline failure details from Compute API responses and sessions.
